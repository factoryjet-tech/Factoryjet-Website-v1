# Three US clusters the demand map has no row for

The Blog Demand Map was built keyword-line-first: seven service lines, keyword volumes under each, topics chosen from the top of the list. That method finds head terms. It cannot see money pages that already exist and have nothing feeding them.

Reading the 161 live US URLs asset-first — start from the pages, ask what content feeds each — produces three clusters with **zero** rows anywhere in the map's 21 US topics. All three have lower search volume than the map's picks and better volume-to-revenue ratios, because the intent is further down the funnel and the destination already exists.

Ordered by return.

---

## 1. Replatforming — 9 money pages, 0 blog posts

**The pages, all live, all unsupported:**

```
/replatforming
/replatforming/magento-to-shopify
/replatforming/woocommerce-to-shopify
/replatforming/squarespace-to-shopify
/replatforming/wix-to-shopify
/replatforming/wordpress-to-shopify
/replatforming/bigcommerce-to-shopify-plus
/replatforming/netsuite-suitecommerce-to-shopify-plus
/replatforming/salesforce-commerce-cloud-to-shopify-plus
```

Nine commercial pages aimed at the single highest-intent moment in ecommerce — a business that has *already decided to move platforms* and is choosing who moves them. Supported by nothing.

Migration searchers are not researching a category. They have a budget, a deadline, and a platform they have already given up on. Volumes are modest; the conversion rate is not comparable to anything in the map.

**Six posts, roughly in order:**

| Post | Intent | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| What a Shopify migration actually costs, by source platform | cost | `/replatforming` |
| Magento to Shopify: what breaks, what carries over, what you lose | fear | `/replatforming/magento-to-shopify` |
| WooCommerce to Shopify: the full data migration checklist | how | `/replatforming/woocommerce-to-shopify` |
| How to migrate without losing your SEO rankings (the redirect map) | fear | `/replatforming` + `/services/ecommerce-seo` |
| How long a replatform takes, week by week | timeline | `/replatforming` |
| Shopify Plus vs staying on Magento: the three-year cost | comparison | `/comparisons/shopify-plus-vs-magento` |

The SEO-loss post is the highest-value single article in this entire document. Losing rankings in a migration is the number-one fear of every business considering one, it is a question with a genuinely technical answer, and answering it well is a direct demonstration of competence to a buyer with a live budget.

**Also note:** `/comparisons/shopify-plus-vs-magento`, `/comparisons/shopify-plus-vs-bigcommerce` and `/comparisons/headless-commerce-vs-monolithic` are three more unsupported comparison pages in the same funnel.

## 2. Verticals — 13 money pages, ~0 blog posts

```
/services/roofing-seo          /services/law-firm-website-design
/services/dental-seo           /services/real-estate-website-design
/services/healthcare-seo       /services/restaurant-website-design
/services/law-firm-seo         /services/nonprofit-website-design
                               /services/saas-website-design
/services/ai-agents-for-healthcare      /services/manufacturing-website-design
/services/ai-agents-for-real-estate     /services/b2b-website-design
                                        /services/small-business-website-design
```

Vertical service pages are commodity pages — every agency in America has a roofing SEO page — and they are won on proof, not on copy. The map's service-line lens structurally cannot see these, because the demand lives under trade terms (`roofing seo`, `dental marketing`, `law firm seo cost`) rather than under service terms.

**One post per vertical, and it must be the same post each time — the one a competitor cannot copy:**

> *"What SEO actually costs a [trade] business, what the [trade] SERP looks like in [named city] today, and what the three incumbents are doing that you are not."*

Named city. Real SERP. Real numbers. Dated. A generic "SEO for roofers" article is worthless; there are ten thousand of them. A teardown of the actual roofing SERP in Charlotte, with the incumbents named and their tactics described, is a sales asset that closes deals whether or not it ever ranks.

**Start with three, not thirteen.** Pick the verticals where FactoryJet has an actual customer to reference — a real engagement beats a researched one every time, and the case study is reusable on the service page.

**Bonus:** the vertical pages cross with the city pages. `roofing seo` + `/charlotte/seo` is a real query shape (`roofing seo charlotte`) with almost no competent competition anywhere in the US.

## 3. Cities — 68 pages across 34 cities, 26 with no content

Live city×service pages: **24 SEO · 20 ecommerce-development · 19 web-design · 3 ai-agents · 2 ai-automation**.

Blog support exists for **Austin, Charlotte, Denver, Miami, Nashville, Raleigh, Tampa, Boise and NYC**.

**No content at all:**

```
Arlington · Atlanta · Boston · Chattanooga · Chicago · Cleveland · Colorado Springs
Corpus Christi · Dallas · Detroit · Fargo · Houston · Huntington Beach · Lakewood Ranch
Las Vegas · Lincoln · Los Angeles · Phoenix · Portland · Providence · San Diego
San Francisco · Sioux Falls · St. Louis · Seattle
```

This is the cheapest work in this document, because **the template is already proven**. `/blog/web-design-austin-tx-small-business-guide-2026`, `/blog/web-design-charlotte-nc-small-business-2026`, `/blog/web-design-denver-co-small-business-2026`, `/blog/web-design-miami-fl-small-business-2026` and `/blog/web-design-nashville-tn-small-business-2026` all exist and all follow one structure. This is duplication of a working pattern, not invention.

**Priority order — by market size against existing page depth:**

1. **Chicago** — has SEO, ecommerce and web-design pages, zero content
2. **Dallas** — same three, zero content
3. **Los Angeles** — SEO and ecommerce, zero content
4. **Seattle** — web design and ecommerce, zero content
5. **Phoenix** — SEO and web design, zero content
6. **Houston, San Diego, Atlanta, Portland, Boston** — next tier

**One hard rule:** a city post must contain something that is only true about that city — a named local SERP pull, local competitor names, a local price range, a real client. Templated city content with the name swapped is exactly the "city pages for cities you do not serve" that the live `seo-tips` post already tells readers not to do. Publishing it would contradict our own advice on our own blog, which is worse than not publishing.
