# Brief — the missing US paid-media page

**Status:** decision required before `google-ads-cost-small-business-2026` can publish
**Verified 25 Aug 2026:** `/services/google-ads`, `/services/ppc`, `/services/digital-marketing` and `/services/paid-media` all return **404**. There is no paid-media page in the 161-URL US sitemap.

---

## The problem in one paragraph

The Blog Demand Map's Phase 2 is "close the Google Ads gap" — 71,740 raw US volume, $40.84 CPC, an AI Overview on every enriched variant. The demand is real. But the map applied its own rule ("blog traffic converts into a service page or it converts into nothing") only to UAE and Australia. In the US, Google Ads has the same problem: **the biggest uncovered commercial pocket in the dataset has nowhere to land.**

Publishing the post without a destination sends 3,600/mo of high-intent traffic to a dead end and teaches the site's internal link graph that this topic goes nowhere.

---

## Two options. Pick one.

### Option A — launch a paid-media service and the page with it

Ship `/services/google-ads-management`. Post publishes with **[CTA-A]**.

**For it:** the largest uncovered commercial pocket in the US dataset. Paid media is adjacent to everything already sold — the landing pages ads need are literally the web-design and CRO work already on the menu. Cross-sell into every existing SEO and ecommerce client. The `google ads cost` cluster alone is 3,600/mo with a $40.84 CPC, meaning the equivalent paid traffic is roughly **$147,000/month at list**.

**Against it:** it is a new service line, not a content decision. It needs someone who can actually run accounts, or the page is a lie. Percentage-of-spend pricing does not work below ~$2,000/month client budgets, which is a large share of the current customer base.

### Option B — do not sell paid media, and say so

No page. Post publishes with **[CTA-B]**, positioning FactoryJet as the honest party *because* it does not sell the thing.

**For it:** zero delivery risk. The neutrality is a genuine differentiator on a query owned by PPC agencies with an obvious stake in the answer. The CTA still converts — into the landing page and SEO work that ad accounts are usually failing on, which FactoryJet already sells. Blog traffic still lands somewhere real: `/services/small-business-seo`.

**Against it:** leaves the largest US pocket on the table, and the post will rank less well without a matching commercial page reinforcing topical depth.

---

## Recommendation

**Option B now, Option A only if a real operator is hired.**

A service page for work nobody can deliver is worse than no page. Option B is publishable this month, is honest, converts into pages that already exist and already rank, and costs nothing. If paid media later becomes a genuine service, the post's CTA is one block swap away — which is why both variants are written into the draft.

Do not compromise by shipping a thin `/services/digital-marketing` page as a catch-all. It would be the weakest page on a site whose 161 URLs are otherwise specific, and it would compete with `/services/seo` for the same intent.

---

## If Option A is chosen — page spec

**URL:** `/services/google-ads-management` (not `/services/ppc` — the query volume is behind "google ads"; not `/services/digital-marketing` — too broad, and it collides with the existing SEO pages).

**H1:** name the outcome, not the channel. The house pattern is `/services/ai-agent-development`'s *"AI agents that work your queues, not a demo"* and `/services/small-business-seo`'s *"Small business SEO that brings you more customers."* Something in that register — a promise plus a disavowal of the industry's default failure.

**Must answer, above the fold:** who it is for (US SMB, $500K–$5M), what the minimum viable spend is, what management costs, and what happens in week one. The existing service pages all commit to specifics; a vague paid-media page would be conspicuously off-brand.

**Sections:** the unit-economics test (reuse the backwards math from the post — customer value → close rate → page conversion → click cost); what is included, itemised; what is explicitly not included; the landing page component, because it is the actual differentiator and it is work FactoryJet already does; reporting, framed as cost per lead rather than impressions; pricing posture consistent with the rest of the site (the existing pages do not publish package grids — do not start here).

**Internal links in:** the Google Ads post, `/services/small-business-seo`, `/services/seo`, `/blog/seo-cost-small-business-2026`, `/pricing`, `/services`.

**Then, and only then, Phase 2 of the map becomes available:** one Google Ads cost post per market — US (drafted), UK, India, UAE. The UK and India posts have the same landing-page dependency; check `/uk` and the India sitemap before committing to those too.
