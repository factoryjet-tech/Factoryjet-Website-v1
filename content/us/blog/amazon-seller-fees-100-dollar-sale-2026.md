## Key Takeaways

1. On a $100 order in most categories the referral fee is **$15**, and that is the number everybody knows. It is rarely the number that hurts.
2. Referral fee percentages are **published and fixed by category** — 8% on computers, 15% on home and kitchen, 17% on clothing over $20, 45% on Amazon device accessories.
3. **Fulfillment is the variable that decides your margin**, it depends on the size and weight of your specific unit, and it is the one number you must pull for your own SKU rather than read in an article.
4. Four costs get left out of nearly every "Amazon fees" article: returns processing, storage on inventory that is not selling, advertising you now cannot switch off, and the plan fee.
5. A product with under roughly 40% gross margin before Amazon touches it will usually not survive FBA. Run the numbers before you send inventory, not after.

## Quick Answer

**On a $100 Amazon order in a standard category, Amazon takes a 15% referral fee — $15 — plus a fulfillment fee if you use FBA, which typically runs several dollars and depends entirely on your unit's size and weight.** A Professional selling account adds $39.99 a month regardless of volume. Realistically, most FBA sellers see Amazon take **25–40% of the sale price** once fulfillment, storage, returns and advertising are counted. The referral fee is the part everyone quotes and the smallest part of the problem.

## The four numbers, in the order they hit you

### 1. The referral fee — published, fixed, per category

This is Amazon's commission. You pay a percentage of the **total price** — which includes the item price, the shipping you charged, and any gift-wrap charge — or a minimum amount, whichever is greater. The minimum is $0.30 in most categories.

Two traps in that table that cost real money:

**Your fee category is not the category shoppers see.** Amazon states this explicitly. A product that browses under "Home & Kitchen" may be billed as something else. Check the fee category, not the browse node.

**Tiered categories punish the price point just above the break.** A $21 shirt is billed at 17% while a $20 shirt is billed at 10%. That dollar of list price costs you $1.57 in fees. Apparel sellers who have never mapped their catalogue against those breakpoints are usually leaving money on the table.

### 2. The plan fee — $39.99, or $0.99 a unit

**Professional:** $39.99 a month, unlimited listings, plus access to bulk tools, Automate Pricing, Amazon Ads, Amazon Business B2B selling, Brand Registry features and APIs.

**Individual:** $0.99 per item sold, no monthly fee, and none of the above.

The breakeven is about 40 units a month. Below that, Individual. Above it, Professional — and if you intend to advertise or use Brand Registry at all, Professional immediately, because Individual cannot.

Note what this does to the per-order math: at 100 orders a month the plan costs you 40 cents an order. At 10 orders a month it costs $4.00 an order. Low-volume sellers systematically under-count this.

### 3. Fulfillment — the number that actually decides it

If you use **FBA**, Amazon picks, packs and ships the order and handles customer service and returns. The fee is charged per unit and is set by the size tier and shipping weight of your specific product.

What matters more than the exact rate is the shape of the thing: **fulfillment is charged on dimensional size and weight, not on price.** Which produces the single most important consequence in Amazon economics —

**A $12 product and a $120 product of identical size cost the same to fulfil.** The referral fee scales with price. Fulfillment does not. So fulfillment is a rounding error on a $120 item and can be most of the margin on a $12 one. This is why "sell cheap stuff on Amazon" fails so reliably, and why the same catalogue can be profitable on its premium SKUs and losing money on its entry-level ones without anybody noticing, because the P&L is reported in aggregate.

If you fulfil yourself (**FBM**), you pay no FBA fee and you eat the actual shipping, packaging, labour and returns instead. That is often cheaper for large, heavy or slow-moving items and almost always worse for anything that benefits from Prime delivery speed.

### 4. The four costs nobody quotes you

**Returns.** Amazon's customer-friendly returns policy is a feature for shoppers and a cost line for you. On FBA you may be charged a returns processing fee in high-return categories, you pay to have returned units inspected and either restocked or disposed of, and unsellable returns are a total loss. Apparel is the category where this most often turns a profitable SKU negative. [VERIFY current returns processing fee thresholds before publishing.]

**Storage.** Charged monthly per cubic foot on inventory sitting in Amazon's warehouses, higher in Q4, with additional charges on inventory that has aged past a threshold. Storage is a tax on bad forecasting. A slow SKU can accumulate more in storage than it earns in margin. [VERIFY current storage rates and aged-inventory thresholds.]

**Advertising.** Not a fee, and functionally not optional. Organic visibility on a competitive listing without ad support is thin, and once competitors bid on your listing the choice is spend or lose the placement. Sellers commonly run advertising at 10–20% of revenue. **That is larger than the referral fee** and it is missing from almost every "Amazon fees" article on the internet.

**Your time.** Listing hygiene, suppressed listings, buy-box loss, account health, suspension appeals. Real, uncosted, and the reason management services exist.

## What to do with this

**Map your catalogue against the fee table.** Specifically the tiered categories — apparel, beauty, baby, grocery, furniture, electronics accessories, jewelry. Find every SKU priced just above a breakpoint. Some of them should move down.

**Pull fulfillment fees per SKU, not per catalogue.** Size tier is decided by the packaged unit. A repack that drops a product into a smaller tier is the cheapest margin improvement available in this entire article, and it is a packaging decision, not a marketing one.

**Cost returns by SKU, not in aggregate.** One high-return SKU hiding inside a healthy category average is the most common thing we find on an Amazon audit.

**Count advertising as a cost of sale.** Not as a marketing budget. It behaves like a fee, so model it like one.

**Then ask whether Amazon should be the only channel.** [Walmart Marketplace](/services/walmart-marketplace-agency), [TikTok Shop](/services/tiktok-shop-agency), [Target Plus](/target-plus-marketplace) and [Faire](/faire-wholesale-marketplace) have different fee structures and different competitive density. Some products that lose money on Amazon make money elsewhere. That is a calculation, not an opinion — and the calculator above runs all of them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What percentage does Amazon take from a sale?**
The referral fee is 8% to 45% depending on category, and 15% in most. On top of that, FBA sellers pay a per-unit fulfillment fee based on size and weight, $39.99 a month for a Professional plan, and in practice 10–20% of revenue on advertising. Most FBA sellers see 25–40% of the sale price go to Amazon in total.

**How much is the Amazon referral fee?**
It varies by fee category: 8% on computers and consumer electronics, 12% on automotive and industrial, 15% on home, kitchen, toys, sports and most other categories, 17% on clothing over $20, and 45% on Amazon device accessories. The minimum is $0.30 per item in most categories.

**Does Amazon charge the referral fee on shipping?**
Yes. The referral fee is calculated on the total price, which includes the item price plus any shipping or gift-wrap charges you collect. Setting a low item price and a high shipping charge does not reduce the fee.

**What is the difference between the $39.99 and $0.99 plans?**
The Professional plan is $39.99 a month with unlimited listings, bulk tools, Automate Pricing, Amazon Ads, B2B selling and Brand Registry features. Individual is $0.99 per item sold with none of those. Breakeven is around 40 units a month, but if you want to advertise you need Professional from day one.

**How much are Amazon FBA fees?**
The FBA fulfillment fee is charged per unit based on the size tier and shipping weight of your packaged product, not its price. Because it does not scale with price, it is trivial on expensive items and can consume most of the margin on cheap ones. Pull the current rate for your specific SKU in the Revenue Calculator.

**Is FBA worth it compared to shipping myself?**
FBA usually wins on small, light, fast-moving items where Prime delivery drives conversion, and on anything where you cannot match Amazon's shipping speed. Self-fulfilment usually wins on large, heavy or slow-moving items, and on products with high return rates where you want control of the returns process.

**What Amazon fees do people forget about?**
Four: returns processing and the loss on unsellable returned units, monthly storage on inventory that is not moving, advertising that is functionally mandatory on competitive listings, and the monthly plan fee spread across a small order count. Advertising alone is often larger than the referral fee.

**How do I calculate my Amazon profit per unit?**
Start with the sale price. Subtract the referral fee for your fee category, the FBA fulfillment fee for that specific unit, the plan fee divided by your monthly order count, your advertising spend per unit, and an allowance for returns and storage. Then subtract COGS and inbound shipping. Our marketplace fee calculator does this across channels.

**Is there a free Amazon fee calculator?**
Amazon's own Revenue Calculator estimates fees for a specific product inside Seller Central. Our free marketplace fee and margin calculator does the same and compares Amazon against Walmart WFS, Target Plus, Faire and your own Shopify store side by side, which is the comparison that actually changes decisions.

**What margin do I need to sell profitably on Amazon?**
As a rule of thumb, under roughly 40% gross margin before Amazon fees, FBA is difficult. Between 40% and 60%, it works with disciplined advertising and good size-tier management. Above 60%, you have room to be wrong about several things. These are guidelines, not guarantees — run your own SKU.

**Does Amazon charge a fee for returns?**
In some categories Amazon charges a returns processing fee on FBA orders once a product's return rate exceeds a threshold. Separately, you always bear the cost of returned units being inspected and either restocked or disposed of, and unsellable returns are a full loss.

**What are Amazon storage fees?**
Monthly charges per cubic foot on inventory held in Amazon fulfillment centres, higher in Q4, with additional charges on inventory that has aged past a threshold. They are effectively a penalty on over-forecasting. A slow-moving SKU can accrue more in storage than it earns in gross margin.

**Do I have to advertise on Amazon?**
Technically no. Practically, on any competitive listing, yes. Sponsored placements sit above organic results, competitors can target your listing directly, and new products have no ranking history to trade on. Budget 10–20% of revenue and treat it as a cost of sale rather than a marketing experiment.

**Are Amazon fees different for books and media?**
Yes. Media items — books, DVDs, music, software and video — carry the 15% referral fee plus an additional $1.80 closing fee per item. On a low-priced book that closing fee can be a substantial share of the sale.

**Why is my Amazon fee category different from what shoppers see?**
Amazon assigns a fee category that does not always match the browse category customers navigate. It is decided by the product's attributes and Amazon's own fee category guidelines. Check the fee category directly — sellers routinely budget from the browse category and get a different bill.

**Can I reduce my Amazon fees?**
You cannot negotiate the referral fee. You can reduce the total: repackage to drop into a smaller size tier, reprice around tiered-category breakpoints, improve inventory turns to cut storage, cut return rates with better listing accuracy and sizing information, and tighten advertising targeting. Size tier and returns are usually the biggest wins.

**Do Amazon fees change?**
Yes, and usually with limited notice. Referral percentages, fulfillment rates, storage rates and surcharges are all revised periodically, and Q4 carries seasonal increases. Re-check your unit economics at least twice a year, and always before committing to a large inbound shipment.

**Is selling on Walmart or TikTok Shop cheaper than Amazon?**
The fee structures differ enough that the answer is product-specific rather than general. Referral rates, fulfillment models and advertising dynamics all vary by marketplace. Run the same SKU across each in a calculator before assuming any of them is cheaper — some products that lose money on Amazon are profitable elsewhere.

**Should I sell on Amazon at all?**
If your product has healthy margin, benefits from Prime delivery, and you can defend the listing, yes. If the margin is thin, the item is bulky and cheap, or your brand depends on owning the customer relationship, a direct store plus a narrower marketplace mix is often the better business. Run the numbers first.
