> "Everybody gets a quote for building the site. Almost nobody gets a quote for owning it. Here is every line that recurs, three real monthly budgets, and the four costs that only appear in year two."

## Key Takeaways

1. A small business brochure site realistically runs **$25–$120 a month** in pure platform costs. The number people are shocked by is not that one — it is the maintenance line underneath it.
2. A Shopify store starts at **$39/month** ($29 billed yearly) and almost never stays there. Apps are the reason.
3. **Renewals are not the price you paid.** Domain, SSL where it isn't free, and annual plugin or theme licences all re-rate at year two, and the promotional first-year price is the one you remember.
4. The single largest recurring cost on most business sites is not hosting. It is **the hours someone spends keeping it working** — and if nobody is being paid for those hours, they are not happening.
5. Deferring maintenance is a loan. You repay it as an emergency rebuild, and the interest rate is brutal.

---

## Quick Answer

**Expect $25–$120 a month to run a small business brochure website, $80–$400 a month for a small ecommerce store, and $300–$1,500+ a month for a custom or high-traffic site.** That covers domain, hosting or platform fees, security, backups, licences and apps. Ongoing maintenance — updates, fixes, content changes and monitoring — is separate and typically adds $100–$500 a month for a small business, either as an agency retainer or as somebody's salaried hours.

*(This article is about running costs. If you are trying to budget for building a site in the first place, [how much a website costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-small-business-usa-2026) is the one to read.)*

## Every line that recurs

### Domain — $12 to $60 a year

Small, but it is the line that takes companies offline. Two rules: **turn on auto-renew**, and **make sure it is registered to the company, not to a former employee or the agency that built the site.** We have watched a business lose its domain and its email because a contractor's card expired in 2023.

Premium and non-`.com` extensions renew higher than they register. The $0.99 first-year offer renews at the real price.

### Hosting or platform — the biggest visible line

| What you are running | Typical monthly | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Shared hosting | $3–$15 | Fine for a brochure site with modest traffic. Not fine for a store. |
| Managed WordPress | $20–$100 | Updates, staging, backups, caching, competent support |
| VPS or cloud (self-managed) | $20–$200 | Control, and you own every problem |
| **Shopify Basic** | **$39/mo, or $29 billed yearly** | Store, hosting, PCI compliance, card rates from 2.9% + 30¢ |
| **Shopify Grow** | **$105/mo, or $79 yearly** | Lower card rates (from 2.7% + 30¢), more staff accounts |
| **Shopify Advanced** | **$399/mo, or $299 yearly** | Custom reporting, third-party shipping rates |
| **Shopify Plus** | **from $2,300/mo** | Enterprise; card rates from 2.25% + 30¢ |
| Modern hosting for a custom build (Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare) | $0–$150 | Free tiers are real; costs scale with traffic and functions |

*Shopify figures verified on shopify.com/pricing, 25 August 2026. [Re-verify on publish.]*

The mistake here is one-directional and predictable: businesses buy $4 shared hosting for a site that took three months to build, then wonder why it is slow. Hosting is the cheapest performance improvement available and the last one anybody makes. If you want to see what your current speed is costing in lost conversions, the [ecommerce speed calculator](/ecommerce-speed-calculator) puts a number on it.

### SSL — usually $0, occasionally not

Let's Encrypt is free and automatic on essentially every credible host and platform in 2026. If you are being invoiced $80 a year for a standard SSL certificate, you are paying for something your host gives away. Extended Validation certificates exist and cost real money; almost no small business needs one.

### Business email — $6 to $25 per user, per month

Google Workspace or Microsoft 365. Not technically the website, always in the same conversation, and always forgotten in the budget. Five users on Workspace is a real recurring line, not a rounding error.

### Plugins, apps, themes and licences — the line that quietly grows

This is where budgets actually break, and it breaks differently on each platform.

**WordPress:** premium theme (~$50–$100/yr), a page builder (~$50–$100/yr), forms, security, backups, SEO, caching — each $50–$200 a year. A normal small business site carries 4–8 paid licences. Call it **$300–$800 a year**, and remember that letting a licence lapse means no updates, which means a security hole.

**Shopify:** the plan is the floor, not the price. Reviews, email, subscriptions, upsells, inventory sync, page builder — $10–$50 a month each. **A typical small store runs $50–$200 a month in apps on top of the plan.** Merchants routinely pay more in apps than in Shopify.

**Custom builds:** fewer licences, but you now pay for the services the platform used to include — search, forms, image CDN, error monitoring, analytics.

Once a year, list every recurring charge and cancel what nobody has opened. Every business we audit has at least one.

### Backups and monitoring — $0 to $50 a month

Some hosts include real backups. Many include "backups" that are a single overwritten copy, which is not a backup. You want off-site, versioned, restorable, and **tested** — an untested backup is a belief, not a backup.

Uptime monitoring is free at the basic tier and there is no excuse not to have it. Finding out from a customer is the expensive way.

### Transaction fees — a percentage, not a subscription

On Shopify Basic, from 2.9% + 30¢ per card transaction; from 2.7% on Grow; from 2.25% on Plus. On WooCommerce you pay your gateway's rate directly, commonly around 2.9% + 30¢.

At $10,000 a month in sales that is roughly $290–$300. Which is worth sitting with: **your payment processing very likely costs more than your entire hosting stack.** Nobody optimises it because it is not an invoice, it is a deduction.

### Maintenance — the real number

Updates, plugin conflicts, broken forms, content changes, security patching, speed regressions, the annual "why is the contact form not sending."

- **DIY:** $0 in cash, 2–5 hours a month of an owner's or a marketer's time.
- **A retainer with a developer or agency:** typically $100–$500 a month for a small business site, more for ecommerce.
- **Nobody:** $0 a month, until the year you rebuild.

That third option is the one most businesses actually choose, and it is the most expensive one. A site nobody updates accumulates outdated plugins, a compromised dependency, a broken checkout on one browser, and content that stopped matching the business two pivots ago. Then it gets replaced. We wrote about why the SEO cost of this is worse than the security cost in [why website maintenance matters for SEO](/blog/importance-of-website-maintenance-seo), and what an hour of downtime is actually worth in [the cost of website downtime](/blog/cost-of-website-downtime-calculator).

## Three real monthly budgets

**A brochure site for a services business — WordPress, managed hosting**

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Domain (annual, spread) | $1–$5 |
| Managed WordPress hosting | $25–$40 |
| Plugin and theme licences (annual, spread) | $25–$65 |
| Backups and monitoring | $0–$15 |
| Business email, 3 users | $18–$45 |
| **Platform subtotal** | **$69–$170** |
| Maintenance retainer | $100–$300 |
| **Total** | **$169–$470** |

**A small Shopify store, ~$20k/month revenue**

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Shopify Grow | $79–$105 |
| Apps (6–10) | $80–$200 |
| Domain and email | $20–$50 |
| Transaction fees at ~2.7% + 30¢ on $20k | ~$550 |
| **Platform subtotal** | **~$730–$905** |
| Maintenance and merchandising | $200–$800 |
| **Total** | **~$930–$1,700** |

Note what dominates that table. It is not Shopify. It is the payment processing, and it is the reason plan upgrades pay for themselves at volume: the card rate drops as the plan rises, and there is a crossover point worth actually calculating.

**A custom build — React or headless, moderate traffic**

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Hosting and CDN | $20–$150 |
| CMS or backend services | $0–$100 |
| Search, forms, image CDN, error monitoring | $30–$150 |
| Domain and email | $20–$50 |
| **Platform subtotal** | **$70–$450** |
| Developer retainer | $300–$1,500 |
| **Total** | **$370–$1,950** |

Lower platform cost, higher labour cost. That is the trade a custom build makes, and it is the right trade only if the site is doing something a template genuinely cannot.

## The four costs that only appear in year two

1. **Renewal pricing.** Hosting introductory rates end. Domains renew at list. Annual plugin licences renew at full price or lose their discount. Year two is often 40–60% higher than year one and nobody forecasts it.

2. **Platform version upgrades.** A major PHP version, a Shopify theme architecture change, a framework major release. Occasionally trivial, occasionally a project. It arrives on a schedule you do not control.

3. **App and licence creep.** Every marketing experiment adds a subscription. Very few get cancelled. This compounds silently — the third-year total is always a surprise to the person who signed off on each individual $19/month.

4. **Content drift.** Prices, team, services and case studies all go stale, and a stale site converts worse and ranks worse. The fix is a few hours a month of someone's attention, and it is the one line that never appears on any budget anywhere.

## What you can cut, and what you cannot

**Cut:** paid SSL, page builders you no longer use, duplicate SEO plugins, apps nobody has opened in 90 days, oversized hosting bought for traffic that never arrived.

**Do not cut:** off-site backups, security updates, uptime monitoring, domain auto-renew, or the maintenance hours. Each of those has a failure mode that costs more than a year of the thing you cancelled.

**Actually worth spending more on:** better hosting, because speed is conversion; and a real maintenance arrangement, because the alternative is a rebuild.

## Two questions to ask before you sign anything

**"What will this cost me in month 13?"** Get it in writing. Introductory pricing is the most common surprise in this entire category.

**"Who owns the domain, the hosting account and the code?"** If the answer is your agency, you do not own your website — you rent it. Anyone reputable will put all three in your name without being asked.

For what a *build* costs and what changes the number, [how much a website costs](/website-cost) is our page on it. If your current site costs more to keep alive than it earns, that is not a maintenance problem and a bigger retainer will not fix it — [website redesign](/services/website-redesign) is the honest next conversation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to keep a website running per month?**
For a small business brochure site, $25–$120 a month in platform costs — domain, hosting, licences, backups — plus $100–$500 a month for maintenance if you pay someone. Ecommerce runs higher, mostly because of apps and payment processing rather than the platform fee itself.

**How much does website hosting cost?**
Shared hosting runs $3–$15 a month, managed WordPress $20–$100, and a VPS or cloud instance $20–$200 depending on resources. Hosted platforms like Shopify bundle hosting into the plan fee. Modern static and edge hosts often have genuinely usable free tiers for small sites.

**Is free website hosting good enough for a business?**
For a static brochure site with modest traffic, a reputable free tier from a modern host is genuinely fine. Free shared hosting from unknown providers is not — expect ads, no backups, poor performance and no support. If the site takes payments or generates leads, pay for hosting.

**How much does Shopify cost per month?**
Basic is $39 a month, or $29 billed yearly. Grow is $105, or $79 yearly. Advanced is $399, or $299 yearly. Plus starts at $2,300. Card processing runs from 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic down to 2.25% + 30¢ on Plus, and apps typically add $50–$200 a month.

**Why is my Shopify bill higher than the plan price?**
Apps and transaction fees. A typical small store runs six to ten paid apps at $10–$50 each, and payment processing at roughly 2.7–2.9% of revenue is usually the largest single line. Many merchants pay more in apps than they pay Shopify.

**Do I have to pay for an SSL certificate?**
Almost never. Let's Encrypt certificates are free and issued automatically by essentially every credible host and platform. If you are being billed for a standard SSL certificate, ask why. Extended Validation certificates cost real money and very few small businesses need one.

**How much does website maintenance cost per month?**
For a small business site, $100–$500 a month buys updates, security patching, backups, monitoring and a few content changes. Ecommerce maintenance runs higher. Doing it yourself costs two to five hours a month instead of cash — the work does not disappear, it just moves.

**What happens if I do not maintain my website?**
Plugins go out of date and become security holes, forms break silently, performance degrades, and content stops matching the business. Nothing appears wrong for a while, then several things fail at once. Deferred maintenance is a loan repaid as an emergency rebuild.

**How much do WordPress plugins cost per year?**
A typical small business site carries four to eight premium licences — theme, page builder, forms, security, backups, SEO, caching — at $50–$200 each per year. Budget $300–$800 annually. Letting a licence lapse means no updates, which is a security decision, not a savings decision.

**Do domain names cost money every year?**
Yes. Most `.com` domains renew at $12–$20 a year, and premium or specialty extensions cost more. The heavily discounted first-year price renews at the standard rate. Enable auto-renew and check the domain is registered to your business, not to an employee or agency.

**Is WordPress cheaper to run than Shopify?**
On paper, often yes — but the comparison is unfair. WordPress moves costs from a plan fee to hosting, licences, security and maintenance hours. Shopify bundles hosting, PCI compliance and updates into the plan. Total cost of ownership is frequently closer than the sticker prices suggest.

**How much does a custom-built website cost to run?**
Platform costs are usually low — $70–$450 a month for hosting, services and email — because there are fewer licences. Developer availability is the real cost: budget $300–$1,500 a month. Custom trades platform fees for labour, which only makes sense if the site needs something templates cannot do.

**What are the hidden costs of running a website?**
Renewal pricing after year one, business email, payment processing percentages, subscription creep from marketing experiments nobody cancels, platform version upgrades, and the hours spent keeping content current. Renewal pricing and processing fees are the two that most often surprise people.

**Do transaction fees count as a website cost?**
They should. At $10,000 a month in sales, a 2.9% + 30¢ rate is roughly $300 — very likely more than your entire hosting and licence stack combined. It never appears as an invoice, so it never gets scrutinised, which is exactly why it is worth scrutinising.

**Can I reduce my monthly website costs?**
Yes, in this order: audit and cancel unused apps and licences, drop paid SSL if your host offers free certificates, right-size hosting you over-bought, and consolidate overlapping tools. Do not cut backups, security updates, monitoring or domain auto-renew — each has a failure mode that costs more than the saving.

**How often should a website be updated?**
Security and plugin updates monthly at minimum, ideally applied on staging first. Content — prices, team, services, case studies — quarterly. A structural review annually. A site that goes untouched for two years is not stable; it is accumulating a bill.

**Who should own my domain and hosting account?**
Your business, in your business's name, on your business's payment method, with your business controlling the login. Agencies can have delegated access. If your agency owns the registration or the hosting account, you are renting your website and a relationship breakdown becomes an outage.

**Is it cheaper to rebuild or to keep maintaining an old website?**
Compare annual maintenance plus the revenue cost of poor performance against the rebuild cost amortised over three to five years. Once an old site needs constant fixing, cannot be updated without breaking, or is slow enough to lose conversions, maintenance is the more expensive option.

**What is a reasonable monthly budget for a small business website?**
For a services business: $150–$450 a month covers hosting, licences, email and light maintenance. For a small store: $900–$1,700 a month including apps and payment processing. Anything materially below those and someone is either absorbing the work unpaid or the work is not happening.

**Does a faster website actually save money?**
It makes money rather than saving it. Faster pages convert better and reduce abandonment, and the upgrade from cheap shared hosting to competent managed hosting is usually $20–$40 a month. On a site doing any meaningful revenue, that is the highest-return recurring spend available.
